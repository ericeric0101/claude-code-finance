## Verdict and what matters

Planet builds and operates a large fleet of Earth-observation satellites, selling frequent imagery, analytics, software access and dedicated satellite services. The company has a differentiated daily global archive and recurring-data model, but it is still a growth-stage, capital-intensive business with net losses and high customer concentration.

SEC Q2 FY2027 10-Q, consolidated, six months ended 2026-07-31: revenue grew 51% to $210.2m, including 58% Q2 growth; RPO was $753.1m. Defense & Intelligence supplied 67.7% of H1 sales and most of the growth.

SEC Q2 FY2027 10-Q cash-flow statement: reported H1 FCF was $21.3m, including a $47.1m deferred-revenue inflow; removing that working-capital timing benefit makes FCF negative. Cash and short-term investments were $865.4m, with liquidity partly supported by ATM issuance and warrant exercises (SEC Q2 FY2027 8-K, filed 2026-09-03).

**Research role:** Growth / Speculative. Strong secular demand and a valuable dataset, offset by government-contract concentration, execution and launch risk, negative GAAP earnings, substantial SBC and an active equity-issuance program. FT Markets, NYSE close on 2026-10-09: $17.18/share. SEC Q2 FY2027 10-Q share count as of Aug. 27: 363.8m. Calculated basic equity value is about $6.25bn. The EV bridge is about $5.9bn after adding notes and leases and deducting cash/investments (source: SEC Q2 FY2027 10-Q; calculated). FY2027 midpoint revenue guidance is $435.5m, implying about 14.3x market cap / sales and 13.5x EV / sales (source: SEC Q2 FY2027 8-K; FT Markets price history; calculated).

## Business, sector and situation

**Facts:** Planet’s fleet provides high-frequency imagery and change-detection data; the company also sells analytics, software access, and satellite hardware/services. Subscription imagery can be recognized over the contract term; dedicated satellite delivery and services can create lumpy revenue as customer acceptance and mission milestones occur. Company-wide recurring ACV was reported at 98%, but Planet’s definition excludes satellite-services contracts and certain self-service users ([SEC Q2 FY2027 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836833/000119312526381874/pl-ex99_1.htm)). The high recurring percentage therefore does not eliminate delivery timing or renewal risk.

SEC Q2 FY2027 10-Q, Note 3, H1 FY2027 customer mix: Defense & Intelligence $142.4m (67.7%); Civil Government $33.7m (16.0%); Commercial $34.2m (16.3%). The Defense & Intelligence increase was $63.8m of total $70.6m growth. Three customers represented 13%, 12% and 11% of H1 revenue. This concentration can drive rapid growth when programs expand, and equally rapid slowdowns if awards, budgets, delivery acceptance or renewals move.

**Sector:** Commercial space / Earth-observation data and geospatial intelligence, with software-like recurring revenue and satellite-manufacturing / infrastructure economics. The closest listed comparison found is BlackSky (NYSE: BKSY), whose business emphasizes tactical high-resolution intelligence; it is not a clean multiple peer. Maxar is privately held and has no current public multiple. P/E is unusable while GAAP earnings are negative. EV/EBITDA is a weak anchor because EBITDA excludes recurring SBC, depreciation on satellites and replacement capex. I use a five-year scenario DCF with a cash-flow margin ramp, cross-checked against forward revenue and steady-state margin assumptions.

**Situation:** Loss-making growth company building satellite capacity. Standard mature-company ROIC and earnings multiples are not yet decision-useful; returns on incremental capital cannot be judged until investment and customer contracts mature.

**Cash-flow normalization:** [Calculation] Remove the $47.1m deferred-revenue inflow from H1 CFO, leaving roughly $21.3m CFO and **negative $25.8m FCF** after capex. Also removing the $23.9m lower-receivables benefit leaves roughly negative $2.6m CFO and **negative $49.7m FCF**. These are timing sensitivities, not a claim that deferred revenue is abnormal or that the amounts forecast future cash. They show why one half-year of reported FCF is not yet a reliable normalized owner-earnings base. FY2027 capex guidance of $100–115m against adjusted EBITDA guidance of only $3–10m suggests heavy continued reinvestment.

**Backlog quality:** 46% of RPO (68% within 24 months) is expected to convert in the next year; backlog conversion is 50% and 70%. The backlog also includes $61.7m cancellable contract value. Government work can include termination-for-convenience rights and appropriations conditions; unexercised options are excluded. Do not treat backlog as guaranteed revenue. The decrease since January is a monitor item, especially with the high government contribution.

**Liquidity and capital structure:** Cash plus short-term investments were $865.4m at July 31. The company has $460m principal of 0.50% convertible notes due October 2030, plus $50.4m lease liabilities. Its conversion condition was triggered for the Aug. 1–Oct. 31, 2026 window. Cash appears sufficient for near-term operations, and solvency is not the main risk in this report. However, liquidity increased in part through $120.8m net Q2 ATM proceeds and $107.8m warrant-exercise proceeds during H1. The June 2026 ATM permits up to $1.5bn in aggregate sales ([SEC Q2 FY2027 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836833/000119312526382016/pl-20260731.htm)). This is available capacity, not necessarily planned issuance, but it makes future per-share dilution a meaningful valuation variable.

**Equity compensation:** H1 SBC was $33.5m, 16% of H1 revenue, versus $26.0m prior year. At July 31, options, RSUs, PSUs and other warrants excluded from diluted EPS totaled about 38.6m potential shares. Those instruments are excluded from diluted loss-per-share arithmetic because the company has a loss, not because they have no economic cost. RSU grants and vested awards, ATM issuance and possible note conversion must be reflected in long-term per-share forecasts.

**Related parties and litigation:** Google owned more than 10% of Planet’s Class A shares and supplied hosting/other services on which Planet recorded $16.1m expense in H1 FY2027; $6.9m remained deferred at July 31. Planet also receives milestone-based Google R&D funding, which offsets R&D expense. The relationship merits tracking as a strategic collaboration and supplier/related-party exposure ([SEC Q2 FY2027 10-Q, Notes 7 and 11](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836833/000119312526382016/pl-20260731.htm)). A Delaware stockholder suit against former officers/directors reached a proposed mediated settlement in May 2026, pending final documents and court approval; Planet remains obligated to indemnify former officers and recorded an accrual. Another acquisition-related arbitration was settled and paid in Q2. No amount for the pending indemnification accrual was separately disclosed in the cited note ([SEC Q2 FY2027 10-Q, Note 8](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836833/000119312526382016/pl-20260731.htm)).

These developments support sovereign-data demand and constellation investment. The German value is a maximum including options, not guaranteed sales, and a demonstration launch does not establish commercial Suncatcher revenue.

**Rates:** On Sep. 16, 2026 the Federal Reserve raised the federal funds target by 25 bp to 3.75–4% ([Fed statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)). **Geopolitics and government spending:** The 10-Q identifies government funding and policy changes, shutdown/continuing-resolution risk, sanctions/export controls and contract termination provisions as risks. Conflict can increase demand for imagery and intelligence, while appropriation delays or changing policy can defer or reduce government revenue. This is a two-sided exposure; no generic geopolitical sales uplift is assumed.

**Oil and launches:** EIA’s October 2026 outlook attributed elevated oil expectations to Middle East supply disruption and forecast Brent at $105/b in Q4 2026 and $84/b in 2027 (source: [EIA October 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/oct26.pdf)). Planet does not disclose a quantified crude-oil sensitivity. Oil could indirectly affect launch, shipping and supplier costs, but the transmission and magnitude are uncertain; no numeric haircut is applied. Monitor capex and gross margin rather than double-counting the issue in cash flows and WACC. This is a targeted event screen, not an exhaustive global scan.

## Valuation and expectations

**Current enterprise value:** [Calculation] Using $17.18, 363.8m Class A and B shares at Aug. 27, and adding $448.3m carrying value of convertible notes and $50.4m leases, then subtracting $865.4m cash and short-term investments, rough EV is about **$5.9bn**. This uses basic shares and does not treat all awards as exercised. At FY2027 midpoint revenue guidance of $435.5m, rough EV/sales is about **13.5x**. Fully diluted per-share values are lower than basic-share values.

**Five-year scenario DCF (analyst assumptions, not company guidance):** FY2027 starts at $435.5m midpoint revenue. The model assumes five-year revenue CAGR, a gradual unlevered FCF margin ramp and terminal EV/FCF multiples. Discount rate is 10.5% base, with 9.5–12.0% sensitivity; this is an analyst assumption, not a quoted company WACC. There is no terminal-growth perpetuity in the base case: a terminal multiple is used because mature cash generation is not established. Add $0.35–0.40bn net liquid assets after convertible debt and leases; use 410m shares to reflect ongoing SBC / dilution. The scenario values are rounded to avoid false precision.

Bear case assumes delayed government conversions, revenue growth that decelerates to low double digits, an 8% FCF margin despite constellation spending, and a lower terminal multiple. Base assumes strong but moderating growth and a difficult transition to 20% FCF margins after substantial reinvestment. Bull assumes success in sovereign services, analytics and international expansion, with 35% growth and 25% FCF margins; it still assumes dilution. A severe downside can include losing most or all of the equity if commercial demand, access to capital or satellite operations fail; the bear value is not a loss floor.

**Cross-checks:** A simplified EV/revenue terminal check gives a base terminal revenue near $1.3bn (25% five-year CAGR). At 20% FCF margin and 25x terminal EV/FCF, the implied terminal EV is about $6.6bn before discounting, broadly consistent with the DCF after discounting and adding interim cash flow. This cross-check is not independent of the DCF assumptions. BlackSky is too different and smaller to justify importing its multiple; Planet’s own historical growth valuation is also distorted by volatile share-price periods and changing constellation investment. P/E is unsuitable due to losses. Reverse-DCF intuition: at roughly 13.5x current FY2027 sales, today’s price requires sustained high growth followed by strong cash margins and successful launches; the existing backlog alone does not support that price.

## Red flags, governance and bear case

No interim audit qualification or material weakness was identified in the reviewed Q2 filing, and the 10-Q reported no material change in disclosure controls. This is not a substitute for a full legal / proxy diligence pass. The specific material concerns are recurring SBC excluded from adjusted EBITDA, concentrated government revenue, declining RPO/backlog, a $1.5bn ATM authorization, $460m convertible notes and multi-class voting rights (Class B has 20 votes per share). No evidence reviewed here establishes improper accounting; rather, non-GAAP EBITDA should be read alongside GAAP loss, capex and dilution.

**Bear case:** Planet’s high growth could be concentrated around a few government programs and satellite-delivery milestones. Contract options, appropriations, cancellation rights and customer acceptance may make large nominal awards less predictable than the headline. If new dedicated constellations cost more or launch later than expected, the company may have to issue more stock before free cash flow matures. Meanwhile, public or government providers and competitors such as BlackSky, Airbus and Vantor can constrain pricing. Under that path, high current revenue multiples could compress even if revenue keeps growing. **Counterpoint:** daily global monitoring, an established archive, 98% recurring ACV by the company’s definition and strong defense demand could build durable, high-margin data subscriptions. The investment question is whether that translates into per-share free cash flow after satellite replacement and SBC.

## What would change my mind

**What must go right:** defense and civil awards renew and convert, commercial subscriptions expand beyond the largest government programs, newer satellites are commissioned on schedule, and margins rise enough for cash flow to cover replacement investment and SBC.

**What can go wrong:** appropriations or contract timing weaken, contract concentration persists, satellite launches or commissioning fail, capex remains high, or further ATM issuance and SBC dilute shareholders faster than FCF grows. Oil and geopolitical disruptions may raise supplier costs without producing enough new paid demand.

**What would change my mind:** two or more quarters of rising RPO/backlog, continued gross margin above 55%, and FCF positive after capex without relying on deferred-revenue inflows would increase confidence. Conversely, missed guidance, consecutive backlog contraction, or annual dilution above 5% without FCF/share growth would lower the valuation and could break the thesis.

**Missing / limits:** Public disclosures do not provide reliable segment capital employed, satellite-level replacement costs, customer renewal terms, quantified oil/launch sensitivity or a complete forecast of diluted share count. The DCF is therefore scenario analysis, not a point forecast. No user holdings were supplied, so no portfolio correlation or holdings overlap is estimated.
