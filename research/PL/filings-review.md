# Planet Labs PBC (NYSE: PL) — SEC/company filing review

**As of 2026-10-11.** This is the filing and operating-data input for the wider research report, not a full valuation. All financial figures are consolidated U.S. GAAP, USD millions unless stated otherwise; FY ends January 31.

## Identity and most recent filings

**Fact:** SEC CIK **0001836833** confirms Planet Labs PBC, NYSE: PL. The latest quarterly report available by the research date is the [Q2 FY2027 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836833/000119312526382016/pl-20260731.htm), for the six months ended July 31, filed September 3, 2026 (accession 0001193125-26-382016). The same-day [8-K, Item 2.02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836833/000119312526381874/pl-20260903.htm) attaches the earnings release (accession 0001193125-26-381874). The latest annual filing is the [FY2026 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836833/000119312526119957/pl-20260131.htm), filed March 2026. The Q2 filing states unaudited consolidated statements; this review does not imply an audit opinion on interim figures.

## Earnings, customer mix and visibility

**Fact:** Q2 revenue was $116.052m (+58% YoY); six-month revenue was $210.202m (+51%). The filing attributes $63.8m of the $70.6m YTD increase primarily to Defense & Intelligence contract expansions and some satellite hardware delivery. YTD customer-type revenue: Defense & Intelligence $142.380m (67.7%); Civil Government $33.668m (16.0%); Commercial $34.154m (16.3%). The current-period growth is therefore exceptionally strong but concentrated in defense and intelligence; hardware milestone revenue can be less smooth than subscriptions. Q2 GAAP gross margin was 56.6% (reported rounded 57%) versus 57.6%; GAAP net loss was $9.353m for Q2 and $148.225m YTD. Most of the unusually large YTD loss reflects a $106.474m non-cash fair-value change in warrant liabilities; this accounting swing is not an operating cash outflow.

**Fact:** RPO was $753.117m at July 31, down $99.318m from Jan. 31 ($852.435m). Backlog was $814.863m, down $85.564m from Jan. 31 ($900.427m), comprising RPO plus $61.746m of cancellable contract value. Management expects to recognize 46% of RPO in 12 months and 68% in 24 months; backlog timing is 50% and 70%. Backlog is not all firm: it includes government contracts with termination-for-convenience rights and written orders without appropriated funding; unexercised options are excluded. The 98% recurring ACV metric is a company-defined figure and expressly excludes satellite-services contract value, so it does not make all current growth equally recurring.

**Fact:** Three customers represented 13%, 12%, and 11% of YTD revenue; in Q2 they represented 17%, 11%, and 10%. Two customers were 25% and 11% of July-end receivables, which are generally unsecured. This is material customer and collection concentration.

## Cash flow normalization, liquidity and dilution

**Fact:** YTD cash from operations was $68.388m versus $85.120m prior year. It includes a $47.083m increase in deferred revenue and a $23.946m cash benefit from lower receivables, alongside a $148.225m net loss, $33.521m SBC, $21.977m depreciation and a $106.474m non-cash warrant-liability fair-value adjustment. Purchases of property/equipment were $44.656m and capitalized internal-use software $2.435m (total capex $47.091m). Company-defined free cash flow was therefore $21.297m (CFO less both capex items); adjusted FCF was $28.8m, which adds back certain legal expenses.

**Calculation / normalization sensitivity:** Removing only the deferred-revenue timing inflow leaves CFO of about $21.3m and FCF of about **negative $25.8m**. Removing both that inflow and the receivables release leaves CFO around **negative $2.6m** and FCF around **negative $49.7m**. These are sensitivities, not alternative GAAP results or forecasts: deferred-revenue growth is a normal part of subscription expansion, but one half-year's cash-flow uplift should not automatically be extrapolated as sustainable underlying free cash generation. Company guidance of FY2027 capex $100–115m, against adjusted EBITDA only $3–10m, also suggests cash generation remains investment-sensitive.

**Fact:** Cash and cash equivalents $415.1m plus short-term investments $450.3m totaled **$865.4m** at July 31, compared with $640.0m at Jan. 31. The 10-Q says operating cash flows plus cash on hand are expected to meet obligations over the next 12 months. This gives meaningful near-term liquidity; a simple “cash runway” burn multiple is not reliable because reported CFO is positive and materially affected by contract billing, warrant accounting and equity financing. The company has $460m principal 0.50% convertible notes due 2030; conditional conversion was triggered for Aug. 1–Oct. 31, 2026, and shares or cash may be used to settle conversion.

**Fact:** Class A + Class B shares outstanding were 363.848m as of Aug. 27, 2026 (10-Q cover: 340.354m A + 23.494m B). During H1, 9.374m public warrant exercises raised $107.8m; these warrants are now extinguished. Q2 ATM issuance sold 3.782m shares for $122.4m gross / $120.8m net, average net price $31.95. The new June 2026 ATM allows up to $1.5bn aggregate offering; this is potential capacity, not an assertion that all will be issued. YTD SBC was $33.521m versus $25.998m prior year; 37.444m options/RSUs/PSUs plus 1.066m warrants were excluded from diluted EPS as anti-dilutive at July 31. The combination of sizeable recurring SBC and active ATM issuance merits explicit per-share dilution treatment in any valuation.

## Guidance and material developments after Q2

**Fact:** In the September 3 earnings release, FY2027 guidance was revenue $430–441m, non-GAAP gross margin 55–57%, adjusted EBITDA profit $3–10m and capex $100–115m. Q3 guidance was revenue $101–105m, non-GAAP gross margin 56–58%, adjusted EBITDA loss $1–6m and capex $30–37m. Management did not reconcile this non-GAAP guidance to GAAP because SBC and D&A are uncertain, and says those expenses will materially affect GAAP results.

**Fact:** After quarter-end, Planet announced (i) an August $8m NGA Other Transaction Authority award for Global Monitoring Service and a German civil-government tender with options and a **maximum** value of €25m over five years, per the 8-K release; (ii) on Sept. 15 a German BMI/BKG award for dedicated tasking, nationwide mosaics and analytics on European-hosted infrastructure, also described as up to €25m over five years including options; and (iii) a successful Oct. 1 launch of 20 satellites including first Project Suncatcher demonstration, Tanager-2 and 18 SuperDoves, with initial contact and commissioning begun for some spacecraft. These expand sovereign-government and product opportunities but the German figure is a ceiling including options, not guaranteed recognized revenue; Suncatcher is a technology demonstration, not a committed commercial revenue stream.

## Risk disclosures and interpretation

**Fact:** The latest 10-Q highlights a history of GAAP losses and uncertain profitability; growth-stage execution and sales-cycle risk; large satellite-services contracts with upfront costs and potential recognition delays; reliance on large enterprise/government buyers; U.S. funding/policy changes and shutdown/continuing-resolution risk; customer concentration; satellite development, launch, deployment and orbital failure; limited suppliers and input costs; competition from commercial entities and subsidized/government systems; export control, sanctions and licensing; macro factors including inflation, FX, tariffs, high rates and geopolitical conflict; AI/cybersecurity; reliance on SBC; and additional equity dilution from the $1.5bn ATM. Government clauses may allow termination for convenience, while reported backlog counts some cancellable contracts. Multi-class shares grant Class B 20 votes/share and give founders control over many shareholder matters.

**Interpretation:** The operating story strengthened, with rapid D&I expansion and better Q2 gross margin/EBITDA, but the highest-quality evidence is mixed: growth concentration is high, both RPO and backlog fell sequentially from fiscal year-end, operating cash flow relies on contract liabilities and collection timing, and cash rose partly from selling stock and exercising warrants. Liquidity is currently ample; dilution and valuation are more immediate concerns than solvency. For valuation, normalize cash generation conservatively, model satellite-services revenue timing and capex, and use a fully diluted share count including the ATM/convertible rather than only basic shares. Do not use the $28.8m adjusted FCF without its definition and legal-expense addback.
