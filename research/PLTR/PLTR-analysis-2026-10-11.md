H1 stock-based compensation (SBC) was $466.8m, 13.1% of sales, and rose 48%. SBC is a real per-share cost even when noncash in the period.

Against FY2026 revenue guidance of $8.150–8.158bn, that is about **61.6x sales**. After subtracting cash and short-term investments of approximately $9.2bn and assuming no debt, enterprise value / guided sales is about **60.5x**. The price is therefore not cheap even if the business continues delivering exceptional growth.

**Research role:** Growth. Critical gates checked: no solvency or accounting qualification issue identified in the latest filing; no current failure in customer demand or cash generation. The platform has high gross margins, strong customer retention/expansion characteristics and a growing commercial footprint; principal risks are extraordinary valuation, dependence on U.S. policy and budgets, customer deployments that may not scale, competition, founder-controlled voting, and recurring SBC dilution. ## Business, catalysts and macro

Palantir sells Gotham (government/defense), Foundry (commercial and public-sector operations), Apollo (software deployment) and AIP (AI application/orchestration). The key long-term question is whether AIP turns pilots and bootcamps into broad, recurring production deployments with high incremental margins. **Fact:** FY2026 guidance is $8.150–8.158bn revenue, at least $3.424bn U.S. commercial revenue (+134%), and $4.5–4.7bn adjusted FCF. Those are strong company targets, but adjusted FCF excludes SBC’s economic cost.

**Short-term thesis:** Q3 guidance calls for $2.160–2.164bn revenue. Track whether U.S. commercial growth and deal conversion sustain the current pace, whether cloud-hosting costs rise slower than revenue, and whether reported GAAP cash generation remains strong after SBC and tax payments. The next quarter’s growth rate will be volatile due to difficult comparisons and contract timing.

**Long-term thesis:** A durable operating-system layer for complex government and enterprise workflows could create high switching costs and let PLTR compound through new modules and users. For the equity, growth must translate into free cash flow per diluted share; headline revenue and adjusted margins alone do not prove this.

**Macro/geopolitical event check through 2026-10-11:** The Federal Reserve raised the policy range to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16. Higher yields weigh on the present value of distant cash flows, especially at a ~60x sales multiple; the scenarios below use 9.5%–12% discount rates and do not add a separate rate penalty. PLTR’s defense and intelligence products may benefit from demand for national security, but appropriations, procurement timing, export rules, sanctions and election/budget changes can delay or constrain awards. Commercial customers may slow deployments in a downturn. The business has no direct oil-price exposure identified in the filings; energy prices matter indirectly through inflation, customer budgets and data-center/cloud costs. No specific event-driven revenue adjustment is made without sourced company-level evidence. The 10-Q itself identifies interest rates, geopolitical uncertainty, trade restrictions and U.S. concentration as exposure channels.

## Financial quality and risks

Balance sheet is unusually strong: June 30 cash/cash equivalents were $2.03bn and marketable securities about $7.19bn, with no debt disclosed. H1 GAAP operating margin improved to 46.7%. H1 net income was $1.942bn, but gains on private and public investments materially lifted non-operating income; operating profit and cash flow are better recurring anchors. H1 SBC of $466.8m equals 13.1% of sales; diluted shares were about 2.403bn at June 30. Future share growth must be monitored alongside cash flow/share. FY2026 company adjusted FCF guide is $4.5–4.7bn, but excluding SBC as a labor cost gives a materially lower owner-earnings figure; this report uses explicit SBC haircuts in its scenario margin.

**Bear case:** U.S. commercial AI pilots fail to convert at scale, growth falls sharply, large government deals slip, cloud costs rise, and SBC continues to dilute holders. A 20% five-year revenue CAGR, 25% year-five owner-cash margin, 25x terminal owner-cash multiple and 12% discount rate produce an indicative ~$40/share (scenario, not a floor); equity can lose its full value. The stock could fall even while revenue grows if its multiple contracts.

I use a five-year owner-cash-flow DCF after treating SBC as an expense, then cross-check the implied sales multiple and reverse expectations. Starting sales are the FY2026 guidance midpoint. Share count expands by scenario to reflect ongoing awards; net cash starts at ~$9.2bn. Terminal multiple approach is used because mature growth is not established. ## Thesis checks

**What must go right:** AIP deployments move from pilots into production, commercial customer expansion continues, gross and operating margins remain high, government programs renew, and diluted-share growth falls below owner-cash growth.

**What can go wrong:** AI deployments prove easy to replicate, commercial demand normalizes, procurement timing or appropriations weaken, cloud costs consume incremental gross profit, investment gains reverse, or SBC and multi-class voting keep common shareholders from capturing per-share growth. High rates or a broader risk-off market can compress the multiple without any immediate operating miss.

**What would change my mind:** I would become more constructive if several quarters show sustained commercial growth with production conversion, GAAP operating margins above 35%, positive cash generation after treating SBC as a cost, and diluted share growth below 2% annually. I would lower the long-term thesis if U.S. commercial growth falls below 40% for two quarters absent a timing explanation, backlog/RDV or customer retention weakens, GAAP margins fall below 25%, or dilution accelerates while owner cash flow/share stagnates. The price becoming cheaper alone would not repair broken conversion or retention.
